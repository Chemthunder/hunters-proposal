#### _weektwo
“My name is Lusiya Wenslow. I serve as a research assistant at the SCP Foundation. It’s been two weeks since SCP-001 was activated. I am 19 years old.

Sorry, just a formality. The director encouraged us to keep logs (diaries) so we stay sane, since we’re going to be here a while. 

I’ve been generally stationed alone in Site-93, in the eastern Antarctic area. Well, me and two others. They only sent two others so the chances of us going insane went down.

They gave us enough food to last for years it feels like, and there’s never a shortage of water, heh. Pros of living in a literal glacier.

Keeping it short this time since I don’t know how long I'm mandated to write.

Bye!”

#### _weekthree
“My name is Dr. Wenslow. I serve as a research assistant at the SCP Foundation. It’s been three weeks since SCP-001 was activated. I am 19 years old.

I got promoted! They said my studies of SCP-001’s effects on the Antarctic area were enough to justify me being promoted to a full-on researcher!!

Even if it was from another continent, I’m still overjoyed. Dopamine hits are few and far between around here.

Some of the food keeps slipping out from the stores, but none that it’s too much of an emergency… yet.

I’ve been studying a new use for SCP-001-B, hopefully either medicinal or some way of making it into a sustainable food source. I hope it proves to be worth my extensive research.”

#### _weekfour
“My name is Lusiya Wenslow. I serve as a researcher at the SCP Foundation. It’s been four weeks since SCP-001 was activated.

That issue with the food has only gotten worse. Almost half is already gone, and no matter what we do it keeps disappearing. We tried having one of us stand guard, or use cameras, but they never saw, somehow.

I haven’t seen or spoken to the rest of the world since I came here, I realize. Even with the entirety of human literature, I feel like I’ve seen it all, and again.

I never introduced you to my colleagues, either. Though, even if I do, they’ll likely be redacted. The SCP Foundation’s all about secrecy, spookiness, why wouldn’t they?

I’ll just refer to them as One (M) and Two (F). That’s name-y enough, right?

In other news, we were also given access to the central cargo depot. If I remember correctly, it was where we came in, and where we will (supposedly) leave. The higher-ups finally are sending us something.

We have to wait a few days / weeks / months for it to get here though. Something about electrical and energy concerns. Hopefully whatever the director(s) want to send us will be useful in the occurring issues.“

#### _weekfive
“My name is Lusiya, I serve as a researcher.

The food situation has dwindled even further, but we have managed to lower the amount of food that’s disappearing. One seemed to find what was causing it but wouldn’t tell both me and Two.

Winter has started, I think. The snow is so thick outside you can barely see. It’s heavier than we predicted, but hopefully shouldn’t interfere with the exterior water pump.

We’ve begun sending signals out into the emptiness. Whether or not they’ll reach someone is unknown.

At least something works.

Better than nothing.“

#### _weeksix
“My name is Dr. Wenslow. I am a researcher at the SCP Foundation. It’s been six weeks since SCP-001’s activation.

At some point, the snow seemed to overtake the external water pump, along with the two backups. All three have gone offline, shutting off all of our water sources. We do have an internal reserve, but most of it is dedicated to showers and the like.

Two proposed we reroute the reserve to solely drinking water, and deal with the consequences later. One originally opposed, but later agreed. It didn’t matter what I voted for.

The cameras near the food vault finally picked up some form of stimuli: a low growling noise. We went over it until our ears and eyes bled, but it seemingly was just a regular distortion from the cold.

Nothing more.

Food is currently at about ~30% of what it should be. Enough to last a few months, instead of the projected ninety years. We have enough water for a few more weeks, hopefully.

The signals we keep sending haven’t met a response yet, outside the default Foundation broadcasts telling humanity of its greater fate.

I haven’t seen a soul outside One and Two in what seems like forever. Feels like I'll go mad down here.“

#### _weekseven
“The food problem finally resolved itself. None of us have any idea why but it stopped, hopefully forever. Water runs lower each day, and I can feel my colleagues' eyes boring into me whenever I run the sink.

The signals still haven’t met a response. One and Two have both given up, and told me to do the same. Almost two months in this hellhole, and I finally met some form of communication.

During a casual Thursday, (of course it’s a Thursday), we received an undocumented signal from an unknown source. I remember the pure despair I felt when it was garbled static none of us could decipher.

Hopefully we’re able to melt the snow outside soon enough for the water issue to be solved. Our stay here now is more about survival than research into SCP-001.“

#### _weekeight
“My name is Lusiya. My name is Lusiya.

It’s officially been two months since we got here. I thought we’d be having a celebration of sorts, but we don’t have enough food or water to really do anything.

My mind keeps flashing back to a research paper I read in our library, the one about Polar Madness. I can already see it in Two.

I hope I live long enough here to go home. To see my mother again. I hope she’s doing well.

Sorry, whoever is reading this. Apparently it’s lightheadedness from the lack of water or something.

The Foundation said we’d receive that shipment at some point. Ticking off the days until that happens is the only thing keeping me sane down here. Feels like I’m starting to forget my own name.

Maybe it was a mistake to live down here.“

#### _weeknine
“The water ran out yesterday. We savored the last of it in a canteen locked away in the lower areas of the Site.

All of our research has been disregarded. SCP-001-B is useless. SCP-001-A has no effects on anything we can find. The broadcasts stopped. Everything is quiet.

#### _weekten
“I have a name. My name is Lusiya.

The only way to remember I have a name is looking at this damn book.

It’s so quiet now. Just our breaths and pen scratches alone with the gentle hum of the lights.

I’m going insane. I keep trying to find a signal from outside, and nobody will respond. My eyes are going to burst from their sockets, my ears are going to explode.

I’m alone. I haven’t spoken to One in days, and Two ended herself out of desperation to leave. I admire her: I’m too much of a coward to do it.

Sometimes I wish I had a religion, like One. Just having a God or something to hope to see in the afterlife would be wondrous.

We still have plenty of food. I think we’ll last until this is over. I’ll make sure of it.

I hope I see you again, Cassie.”

#### _weekeleven
“Hours ticking by, I think. I’ve silenced all the clocks. I finally confronted One, hoping to see another human being. Just to be near one.

He was gone.

I looked everywhere, but he had just vanished. My bet is that he walked outside.

Polar Madness, huh.

That shipment is hopefully soon. I think.”

#### _weektwelve
“I was right. That shipment finally arrived.

The box sat in the cargo depot; just a simple box. I ravenously opened it, hoping for food or water or something to escape this fucking building.

All that was inside was a miniature containment unit for an SCP they wanted to lock up here.

SCP-6399.

That was it.

Just a box of masks and a stapled paper detailing the things.

I’m going to die here.“

#### _weekthirteen
“It’s so quiet here, now that I’m alone. I keep looking at the compartment housing the only firearm we have. 

Cassie wouldn’t want me to keep suffering here. Mom wouldn’t want me to be here.

Shouldn’t be too hard.

I’ll leave this diary of sorts for whoever comes and checks on this place. I’ll leave a note just for the director.

I guess this is goodbye. I think I’ll see Cassie again, wherever I go when this is over.

I love you Cassie.“


# Dr. Wenslow’s Parting Words
“To whoever finds this, and myself.

Hello! I was Dr. Lusiya Wenslow of the SCP-Foundation, researching SCP-001 and its phenomenon at Site-93 with my two colleagues.

Our director sent us here, alone, with decades of food and infinite water. Due to some fun little oversights, both rapidly declined about one month into our stay here. Both of my colleagues died two-ish months in. 

So, to you, the wonderful reader, go fuck yourself. You and the director [REDACTED] both left us here to rot, leaving us at the hands of nature and ourselves.

You promised us that we’d see our families again, and shipped us another fucking object to “research.” I don’t know what we could have ever done to ANY of you to deserve this, but whoever made this choice deserves to burn.

So, to end this little ramble, you will notice I used the emergency gun to end myself, and burned any and all of our research and findings.

I hope I get to see the looks on your faces when I laugh as you descend to hell.

Fuck you, and all that came before you.”
